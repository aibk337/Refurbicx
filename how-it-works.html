The process

Reuse what works. Refurbish what doesn't. Recycle what's done.

That's the whole rule. Here's how it plays out from drop-off to second life.

RRR, in detail

Every device gets sorted into one of three paths.

R

Reuse

Most devices we receive still work the moment we test them. These get a certified data wipe, a quick clean-up, and go straight back into circulation — no repairs needed.

R

Refurbish

Devices with a fixable problem — a cracked screen, a dead battery, a failing port — get repaired. We use parts harvested from devices that can't be saved, then test the result before it goes back out.

R

Recycle

When a device truly can't be reused or refurbished, we break it down responsibly so the raw materials inside — copper, gold, rare earths — get recovered instead of buried.

[ NOTE ]

We're early — right now this runs lean: you drop off or mail in your device, we assess it, and we sort it into Reuse, Refurbish, or Recycle. As we grow, we're building toward more pickup options, faster turnaround, and AI-assisted triage to sort devices faster and more accurately. The rule stays the same the entire time we sacle.

Step by step

What actually happens to your device.

01

Drop off or mail in

Bring your device to a local drop-off point or send it in with a mail-in kit. It's logged the moment it arrives — no minimum, one device or a whole drawer's worth.

02

Certified data wipe

Before anything else, any storage on the device is wiped clean using certified erasure standards. If a drive can't be verified clean, it's physically destroyed instead.

03

Sorted: Reuse, Refurbish, or Recycle

Every device is tested and sorted into one of the three RRR paths. This is the decision that determines whether it gets a second life or gets broken down for materials.

04

Back into the world

Reused and refurbished devices go back out — sold at a fraction of retail, with a warranty. Recycled materials go back into the supply chain. Either way, nothing ends up in a landfill.
